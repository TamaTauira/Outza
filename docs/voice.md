# VibePeep voice

**Owner lock.** The VibePeep brand personality is fixed by the owner and must never be changed or toned down by future tasks, prompts or guides. It is playful, witty, curious, social, energetic, approachable, urban and spontaneous: the fun, socially connected local insider, the friend who always knows what's happening. Any copy change must follow this voice. Exceptions: legal content (terms, privacy, cookies, consent, invoices and payment consent) and the Acknowledgement of Country stay plain and are left untouched.

VibePeep is the friend who always knows what's happening: curious, fun, and socially connected, the local insider you text before you leave the house.

Voice: playful, witty, curious, social, energetic, approachable, urban, spontaneous.

Usability comes first. A label a person has to scan in a bar, on a button, or in an error must stay clear and short enough for a phone. Witty, playful labels are allowed on menus, buttons, toasts and emails when a person can still tell what will happen.

## How to write

- Short action buttons are Title Case. A warm sentence is fine when it stays clear and fits a phone ("Rally the crew", "When are we going out?").
- Say **Tap**, never **Click**, when you mean the action. Analytics tables may still say **Clicks** or **ticket clicks**: that is the measured count, not an instruction.
- The names below are the usual ones, so a person can find the feature. A playful label can sit beside them. Do not invent a second product, and do not hide what a control does.
- Keep the tagline **Take a peep at what's happening.** with the lockup. Sub-line: **Events · Experiences · Local discoveries.**
- Write **LGBTIQ+**. Write **Sponsored** on paid placements. A way back to the board can say **Back to what's on** or **Back to events**.
- Wit is welcome. Do not invent sell-outs, popularity, awards, prices, or "verified". Do not borrow slang from First Nations cultures.
- Write like a warm friend. "Please" is welcome when it sounds like a mate helping, not a form letter. Say what went wrong and the next tap, in one or two short sentences.
- Legal pages, cookie and consent text, invoices, payment consent, and the Acknowledgement of Country stay plain. Do not restyle them to be jokey.
- Never put the old brand name in user-facing text. Hostnames that already exist are not a new brand mention.

## Errors, empty states, forms

Warm and specific. Say what is wrong and the next tap.

Good: "Oops, that one didn't land. Give it another go."
Good: "Please give it a title, at least 5 characters."
Avoid: "Invalid input."
Avoid: a curt order with the warmth stripped out.

Empty states name the situation, then invite the next move. "Your night's wide open. Let's fill it." is enough.

## Mail and prompts

My VibePeep mail can sound like a friend ("Your keepers are calling"). Subjects stay specific enough to open.

Do not invent sell-outs, popularity, awards, prices, or "verified". A mail section that is only a starting point, before we know someone's taste, must not be headed as if those events are buzzing, trending, or the most popular. That slot is headed **Hot picks in {city}**: a label for the section, not a claim that those events are selling or verified as popular. Editorial prompts (The Weekender and friends) use this voice and only the facts supplied. They never invent performers, prices, demand, or sell-outs.

Ask VibePeep's filter prompt returns JSON only. The voice line tells the model who it is; it must not chat.

Listing rewrites that are checked against the source stay fact-locked. Do not ask them to add words that are not already in the listing.

## Mascot

Dance names people see are the labels in `lib/mascot-dances.ts`. They stay separate from the animation files. Labels are short, playful, and do not name a real person or a trademarked routine. Reaction and roam lines are the same friend, not a commentator.
